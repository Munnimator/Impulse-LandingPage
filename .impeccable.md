## Design Context

### Users

ImpulseLog is for neurodivergent adults with ADHD, especially in the high-friction moment when an impulse purchase feels urgent. The core jobs are to pause before buying, record resisted or intentional purchases quickly, make saved money visible, and build repeatable habits without shame.

### Brand Personality

Supportive, empowering, momentum-building. The product should make impulse resistance feel achievable and rewarding rather than clinical, restrictive, or judgmental.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve the current light, playful visual identity, accessible typography, purple/pink accents, rounded forms, and app-led screenshots. The experience should feel calm enough for an overwhelmed user but energetic enough to reinforce progress. Avoid financial-institution severity, medicalized ADHD language, shame-based framing, and unnecessary redesign for novelty.

### Design Principles

1. Reduce friction: important actions and explanations should be quick to find and easy to understand.
2. Reward resistance: frame almost-buys and intentional decisions as visible progress.
3. Stay nonjudgmental: never shame purchases, symptoms, setbacks, or financial circumstances.
4. Design for ADHD accessibility: maintain strong hierarchy, readable type, predictable interaction, restrained motion, and WCAG AA contrast and keyboard support.
5. Preserve recognition: evolve implementation quality without discarding the existing light, playful ImpulseLog identity.
